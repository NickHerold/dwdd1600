Digital Citizenship

What is Digital Citizenship?

Digital citizenship is the responsible, ethical, and safe use of technology and the internet. Just as physical citizenship involves understanding rights, laws, and respectful behavior in a community, digital citizenship applies those same concepts to the online world.

What is Digital Law?

Digital law is the legal framework governing electronic actions, digital technology, and online behavior. It defines the electronic responsibility for actions, ensuring that societal laws and ethics apply to digital spaces.

What is AUP?

An Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) is a set of rules and guidelines implemented by a business, school, internet service provider (ISP), or website outlining how its network, devices, and digital services may be used. It acts as a code of conduct for technology usage to ensure safety, legal compliance, and network integrity.

What is plagarism?

Plagiarism is the act of using someone else's ideas, words, work, or creative output without proper acknowledgment, presenting it as your own original creation.

It is considered an ethical breach in academic, professional, and digital environments.

What is academic dishonesty?

Academic dishonesty is any form of cheating, deception, or misrepresentation that subverts the learning process and undermines the integrity of an educational institution.

It occurs whenever a student or researcher attempts to gain an unfair academic advantage through unethical means.

What are the rules about AI generated content?

The rules for AI-generated content vary depending on whether the context involves copyright law, academic policies, or digital platforms, but they center on three main principles: transparency, human accountability, and intellectual property.
